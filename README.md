# kubewing 🪽

Keyboard-driven native desktop UI for Kubernetes: `kubectl` + grep/watch/diff + tabs/splits. See [Plan.md](Plan.md).

## What works today

- Live table for any resource (`po`, `deploy.apps`, CRDs …) of any kubeconfig context; columns from server-side Table output.
- Columns per resource type: `o` (or a right-click on the header, or "columns" at the right of the status line) lists every server column, `-o wide` ones too (marked `wide`; the `-o wide` switch shows or hides them all), to show, hide (`space`) and reorder (drag & drop, or `⌥↑`/`⌥↓`); custom columns add a field path (completed from the pane's live objects, one key at a time) or a CEL expression over `o` (completed like the filter bar's CEL field; keys with dots, e.g. labels, need CEL), typed as text, number, bytes or age. `⏎` saves, and every pane of that type follows at once, in every context (they're kept by type, e.g. `pods`, `deployments.apps`); an invalid expression is refused in the dialog. In the table itself, a click on a header sorts by its column (ascending, again for descending, a third time back to kubectl's order; numbers sort as numbers, an age column by time (youngest first), durations such as an event's `LAST SEEN` (`5m`, `2d3h`) by their length, empty and `<unknown>` cells last; a triangle in the header shows it; the sort belongs to the pane and its back/forward history, not to the type); dragging a header moves its column (saved for the type the same way); dragging a header edge resizes it, a double-click fits it to the visible rows; the widths belong to the type as well. Columns and widths are kept in `columns.yaml` across restarts; "reset columns" goes back to the server's defaults (so new server columns, e.g. after a CRD upgrade, show again), "reset widths" to the default widths. Changing columns keeps the watch and the diff state.
- A kubeconfig context that sets a namespace starts in it: the first pane at launch (current context) and new tabs/splits, and `⌘O` prefills it when you pick that context. Contexts without one start in all namespaces.
- One watch stream per source (`as=Table;includeObject=Object`): cells and objects come from the same resourceVersion.
- Keyed diff by UID: changed cells, "changed, not visible" marker, added/deleted rows (delete + recreate = remove + add), AGE never counts as a change, noise (resourceVersion, managedFields, heartbeats) ignored.
- Splits/tabs (dockview): drag a divider to resize; `⌘D` split stacked, `⌘⇧D` split side by side (deliberately swapped vs. iTerm), `⌃D` or `⌃⇧D` close the pane, `⌘T` new tab, `⌘W` close the tab (it asks first if the tab holds more than one pane), `⌘1`–`⌘8` go to that tab and `⌘9` to the last (shown in the tab header), `⌘⇧[ ]` cycle tabs (from anywhere, also a shell), `⌘[ ]` cycle panes, `⌘⌥←→↑↓` move to the tile in that direction. `⌘⇧⏎` maximizes the focused pane to the whole tab (as iTerm does) and back; the other panes stay open behind it (their watches and shells keep running), and a small `⤡N` badge at its top right says so (N = panes hidden; its tooltip says more) — a click on it, `⌘⇧⏎` again, moving focus to another pane, a split or closing a pane shows them all again. Per tab; a tab with a single pane has nothing to maximize. `⌃⇥` / `⌃⇧⇥` switch tabs in MRU order: a quick tap flips to the previous tab, and holding `⌃` shows a switcher (`⇥` steps, release commits, `Esc` cancels). `⌃D` stays out of text fields, where it is forward-delete; in a terminal `⌃D` belongs to the shell (ending it closes the pane), and `⌃⇧D` closes the pane of a shell that won't end. Splits on the same source share one watch.
- Names: `⌘⇧I` (or a double-click on the tab) names the tab, `⌘I` (or a double-click on the empty part of a pane's header) the pane, as iTerm's "Edit Tab Title" / "Edit Session". The name applies as you type; `⏎` or a click elsewhere keeps it, `Esc` puts the old one back, an empty name removes it. A pane's name shows as a label in its header, and in the tab while the tab has no name of its own (otherwise the tab shows the focused pane's resource and namespace); a context label (`PROD`) always stays in front. Splits and new tabs start without a name. Names live as long as the window, they aren't saved.
- Moving panes (as in ghostty): moving the mouse over a pane shows a grip at its top (it fades once the mouse rests; there is none while a single pane is all there is). Dragging it shows where the pane would go: the side of another pane nearest to the pointer (it takes half of that pane's place), the whole edge of the tab when close to it, a tab header (the right edge of that tab; holding over a header for a moment shows the tab, so the pane can go next to one of its panes), or `+` (a tab of its own). Dropping moves the pane as it is (its watch, filters, history, a shell and its screen) and focuses it; a tab left without panes closes. `Esc` cancels the drag, back on the tab it started in.
- Filter bar: `/` grep (applies as you type; space-separated terms must all match, `!x` excludes rows containing x, `^x` means a cell starts with x and `x$` that one ends with it (`^Running$`: exactly), a lone `|` between terms means either (`etcd pending | ^crash`: etcd and one of the two), `"a b"` is one term, `\!` `\^` `\$` `\|` are literal; a negated term or an alternative after `|` applies once finished — a space after it, `⏎` or leaving the field — since `!c`, `!co` … would otherwise blank the table and bring rows back as you type (and `a | r` show nearly everything); `Esc` puts back the grep the field had when it got the focus); the matches are highlighted in the cells, except in age columns, which grep sees as timestamps), `l` label selector, `c` CEL filter over the object `o` (`o.status.containerStatuses.exists(c, c.restartCount > 3)`). Labels and CEL are drafts until `⏎`: a half-typed value would match nothing, so while typing the table stays as it is and the field shows what the draft would show (`→ 2 / 3`; `≥` when a wider label selector may match objects not loaded yet), or why it can't apply (parse error with a caret, not a bool). `⏎` or leaving the field applies (an invalid draft stays in the field, red, with the reason as tooltip), empty + `⏎` clears, `Esc` drops the draft (the applied value comes back). `⏎` and `Esc` go back to the table, in all three fields. CEL completes field paths from the pane's live objects (`o.status.` → the keys found there, with type and how many objects have them; also inside `exists(c, c.…)`; a key that isn't an identifier goes in as `["app.kubernetes.io/name"]`, `.?`/`[?` stay optional, and typing `["` completes keys too — Kubernetes' `__dot__` escaping is for typed schemas on the apiserver and doesn't apply to kubewing's schemaless objects), the common functions, and after a field and a space (or on `=`, `!`, `<` …) the operators that fit its type (a string gets `==`, `!=`, `in […]` and the ordering ones, which also order RFC 3339 timestamps; a bool `&&`/`||` first; a map or list only `==`/`!=`). The label selector completes the objects' label keys (with how many objects have them), after a key and a space the operators (`=`, `!=`, `in (…)`, `notin (…)`), and after `=`/`!=` or inside `in (…)` the key's values, most common first; among the source's objects that match the selector's other requirements, so the applied selector doesn't limit them (with `app=web` applied, `app=` offers every app and the keys those pods don't have are there too; when the pane doesn't hold them, a metadata-only list fetches them). In both, the list opens as you type where there is something to complete, and `↓` opens it anywhere (an empty field lists what can start it); `⇥` completes, `Esc` closes the list (a second `Esc` leaves the field), `↑` in an empty CEL field recalls recent filters. Filter changes keep the watch and the diff state. Errors other than a missing field (which just doesn't match) show in the status line.
  - Label selector examples (Kubernetes' syntax, sent to the API server; keywords lowercase): `app=web`, `app!=web`, `env in (prod, staging)`, `env notin (dev)` (also matches objects without `env`), `env` (has the label), `!env` (doesn't), `app=web, env in (prod, staging)` (commas AND).
  - CEL for what a selector can't express (runs in kubewing on the loaded objects, so narrow with the selector first): OR across keys `o.metadata.labels.app == "web" || o.metadata.labels.tier == "db"`, patterns `o.metadata.labels.app.startsWith("web-")` or `.matches("^web-[0-9]+$")`, beyond labels `o.status.containerStatuses.exists(c, c.restartCount > 3)`, `o.metadata.annotations["example.com/owner"] == "team-a"`.
- Hide healthy: `h` (or the `hide healthy` checkbox in the pane header) hides what needs no look. Pods: Running with every container ready (sidecars included, completed init containers ignored). Deployments and StatefulSets: the controller has seen the latest spec (`observedGeneration`), and current, updated, ready and available replicas all equal `spec.replicas`. ReplicaSets: the same without updated. DaemonSets: scheduled, updated, ready and available all equal desired, none misscheduled. Anything terminating stays visible, other kinds aren't affected (no checkbox). It is a pipeline stage (`hideHealthy: true`), evaluated live: an object that becomes healthy drops out, one that stops being healthy comes back.
- `j/k`, `g/G` or `⌥↑/⌥↓` first/last row, `⌘O` open context/resource/namespace (it starts from the pane's view without what a jump set to pick its target: the field selector, which it has no field for, and the CEL filter, e.g. `o.metadata.name == "shop"`, unless you've changed it since; neither would apply to another resource).
- Errors in the status line (a failed list/watch, a CEL filter failing on objects) show their first line; a click opens the full message above it, selectable (`⌘C`) or `copy all`, `Esc` or a click elsewhere closes. When client-go only says "the server rejected our request for an unknown reason", the message goes on with what the server actually answered and its HTTP status.
- Time-travel: `t` in the table shows the pane as it was (starting record history if it's off: the timeline begins then), at the newest change. The status line becomes a timeline: the recorded span with a tick per change of the pane's rows and the moment shown; a click goes there. `←`/`→` go to the previous/next change, `⇧←`/`⇧→` a minute, `Home` to the start; `t`, `Esc`, `End` or the "⏸ 12:03:14" badge in the header go back to live (changes kept arriving meanwhile, counted as "+n since"). In the past every row is the object as it was then (deleted ones back, later ones hidden), with the pane's filters, sort and columns; the cells a step changed are marked. The detail view shows the version of that moment, with the timeline at its bottom (a click moves it). `⇧H` on a line of the YAML (or of the diff's right side) lists the values of the field at the cursor over time: the header says so while history is recorded, and the editor's right-click menu has it as "history of this field" (on the line clicked). `⏎` on a value takes the table there, `Esc` back to the YAML. Each pane has its own moment. With recorded history the row menu's diff entry says how many changes it steps through ("diff changes (7)").
- Object view, in the pane, in a read-only Monaco editor: `⏎`/`y` shows the row's YAML in place of the table (a step in the pane's history: `Esc` or `⌘←` back to the table, with its row and scroll as they were, `⌘→` forward again), `⇧⏎` in a split to the right instead, to compare (a second `⇧⏎` from the table another one; `Esc` at its top closes it, the focus goes back to its table). A split is pinned to its object; its **follow** checkbox makes it show whichever row is selected in its table, as the selection moves (off again: it stays on what it shows). It shares its table's watch and keeps it open, so it stays when the table closes or shows something else (following then stops, the header says why). `d` side-by-side diff of the previous vs. current normalized object (followed as new versions come in). With **record history** on (the status line's "⏸︎ record history", or `⇧R` in the table; off by default, since it costs per event: it's the source's, so all its splits follow, and stopping drops the history) the source keeps its objects' versions (within 512Mi, `history.budget`; past it the oldest go first): `[`/`]` (or the `‹`/`›` buttons) step to older and newer ones, the header shows which two are diffed and when they were seen, and the changed fields are listed above the diff as paths (`spec.containers[name=app].image: "web:1" → "web:2"`), `⇧D` `kubectl describe` (with events — `d` is the diff toggle here, so describe keeps `⇧D` in this view), `⌘F` find, `w` wraps long lines on/off (on by default; holds for the session, in YAML, diff and describe). A held `Esc` (key repeat) stops at the top of an object view: only a fresh press leaves or closes it. In a diff (object or describe) `j`/`k` or the `↓`/`↑` buttons in the header jump to the next/previous change, wrapping around; the header shows which one (`2 / 5`), and the view opens on the first. In the YAML, `=` on a line filters the pane by that field's value and `!` by "not this value" (objects without the field included); fields inside lists become `exists()`.
- Describe diff: in the describe view `d` diffs against the previous look at the same object (by UID, this session), `r` re-runs describe. Unlike the object diff it shows what describe adds (events, counts); relative times (event ages, heartbeats, lease renewals) are blanked and tables re-aligned, so only real changes show. It compares looks, not watch history: changes between two looks are summed up.
- Decode (`⏎` on a line of the YAML, or "decode value" in its right-click menu): the field's value decoded in the object view's place — base64, gzip and brotli as detected (a managed resource's `data.yaml.br` is `base64 → brotli → yaml`), then rendered: a JWT's header and claims with their times (`exp: … # in 3h`, expired ones marked; the signature isn't verified), a certificate's subject, issuer, SANs, validity and fingerprints, a private key's type and the certificate of the same secret it matches (or a mismatch), JSON as YAML, anything binary as a hex dump. The pane's header shows where it is (`api-0 › data["data.yaml.br"] › doc 2: data.kubeconfig`; a click on the object or a level goes back up to it), the chain is above the value; a click on a step leaves it out (the brotli bytes, the PEM text), `↺ auto` goes back; `⌘A` `⌘C` copies the text. Nothing to decode on a line says why in the pane's status line. `⏎` in decoded YAML goes deeper (the kubeconfig in a managed resource's secret, its CA data), `Esc` back up a level and then to the YAML (its cursor and scroll as they were); `⇧⏎` puts the decoded level in a split of its own, to compare it with another. In the diff (`d`), `⏎` decodes the field in both versions and diffs those, `[`/`]` step versions. Decoded text is never kept: each view decodes from the object again.
- Text in a cell selects as in a text field: double-click a word (a name, IP, image or `key=value` is one word: spaces, commas and brackets separate), triple-click the whole cell, drag over a part of it (the drag stays in the cell it started in). `⌘C` copies the selection; an age cell is always selected whole and copies its timestamp (`2026-01-01T10:00:00Z`), not the `5m` it shows. `Esc`, a click elsewhere or another row clears it; a live update that changes the cell drops it too (`⌘C` then says so, rather than copying something else). A double-click doesn't open the YAML: `⏎` or `y` does.
- Object actions: `⌘C` without a text selection copies the selected row's name (in text fields, the YAML view and terminals `⌘C` is the normal copy), `⌘⇧C` any other cell of it: a picker lists the row's columns with their values, then the whole row (tab-separated, so it pastes into a spreadsheet's columns); an age column copies its timestamp, not `5m`; empty cells are skipped; `j/k` move, `⏎` copies, `Esc` cancels. The column copied last is highlighted again (so `⌘⇧C ⏎` copies the same column of the next row); a right-click on a cell and "copy cell…" in its menu highlight the cell clicked, `d` describe (`⇧D` in a split to the right), `⌘⌫` or `Del` delete (UID precondition, so a recreated object of the same name is never hit), `x` exec into a pod (bash, else sh) in a terminal pane below (xterm.js; every key goes to the shell except `⌘` chords, `⌃⇥` and `⌃⇧D`; `⌃D` or `exit` ends the shell and the pane goes with it, `⌘W` closes the tab as anywhere else; `⌘F` finds in the output and scrollback as Monaco's find does: matches highlight as you type, `⏎`/`⇧⏎` or `⌘G`/`⌘⇧G` step (also from the shell), `Aa`/`ab`/`.*` (`⌥⌘C`/`⌥⌘W`/`⌥⌘R`) toggle case, whole word and regex, `Esc` closes back to the prompt and `⌘F` reopens with the last query; a session that never started, or a shell that exits non-zero before the first keystroke, keeps its pane so the reason stays readable, and `⌃D` closes it once read), `f` port-forward on 127.0.0.1: the dialog lists the pod's running forwards (✕ stops one; from the keyboard `⇧⇥` goes up into them, `↑`/`↓` pick, `x` or `⌫` stops, `⇥`/`Esc` back) and offers the declared ports not forwarded yet, each locally on the same port, except below 1000 (binding needs root), which gets 8000 added (`8053:53`, `8443:443`) — one is pre-filled; with several, a list shows each with its name, protocol and container (type a number or name to narrow it; `⏎` adds the highlighted one and then nothing is highlighted, so the next `⏎` forwards: one port is `⏎ ⏎`, another one `↓ ⏎` in between; `⇥` completes to the best match). UDP-only ports are listed but can't be picked: port-forward carries TCP only, as with kubectl. Each port is its own forward, listed and stopped on its own. If one fails (a typed local port below 1024, a port in use), the others still start; the failed ones stay in the field with their error, and the port list closes so the error stays readable (typing opens it again). The `⇄ N` badge in the tab bar (or `⌥⌘P`) lists all forwards, also those whose pod is no longer shown; `↑`/`↓` pick, `x`, `⌫` or ✕ stops one. With more than one container to enter, `x` asks which one: the pod's containers, then init, sidecar and ephemeral ones, each with its state; ones that aren't running are listed but skipped. The default (`kubectl.kubernetes.io/default-container`, else the first container) is highlighted, so `x ⏎` enters it; `j/k` move, `Esc` cancels. A pod with a single one goes straight to its shell. A confirmation, if the rule asks for one, names the container. `b` starts a debug container in the pod (`kubectl debug -it`): a dialog asks for the image (prefilled from `debug.image` in `config.yaml`, else the last one that started; `↓` lists the configured one and the last 10 that started, typing narrows them; ✕ on a history entry forgets it), the container whose processes it shares (the pod's default; empty = none) and the profile (`general`, `baseline`, `restricted`, `netadmin` for tcpdump, `sysadmin` for privileged; default from `debug.profile`). All three are completion fields as in `⌘O` (`↓` lists the choices, `⇥` completes); a target or profile that isn't one of them is refused in the dialog. `⏎` on a highlighted suggestion only takes it into the field; the next `⏎` starts, from any field. The shell opens in a pane below, with the image and target in its header; until the container runs, the pane shows its waiting state and the kubelet's events for it (`Pulling image …`, `Pulled …`), and a container that can't start (bad image) keeps its pane with the reason. Like `kubectl debug`, the ephemeral container stays in the pod (terminated) until the pod is gone.
- Logs (stern-like, multi-pod tail): `⌘L` on a row first asks what to show, then opens a log pane below that follows its pods — a pod row follows that pod (by name, so a recreated StatefulSet pod carries on), a workload/node/namespace row follows all of its pods, keeping up as pods come and go. `⌥⌘L` (or "logs of pane") follows every pod the pods pane currently shows, with its namespace, label selector, grep and CEL filters applied (a snapshot: later edits to the pane don't change a running tail). The dialog lists the containers of those pods (all checked; unchecking some follows only the rest), the history per container — the last lines (`100`, prefilled from `logs.tail`), since a duration ago (`10m`, `1h30m`, as kubectl's `--since`) or `all` — and follow (on) and timestamps (off); `⏎` starts from anywhere in it, so `⌘L ⏎` is the defaults, `Esc` cancels. Without follow, each container's log is read once (the pods there at the start) and the tail ends. Each line is prefixed with what tells it apart, colored per pod: `pod/container`, just the container when all lines come from one pod, just the pod when every pod has a single container of the same name, nothing for a single container; the column fits the longest label (up to 40 characters; longer ones are cut in the middle, keeping their end) and the live filter still matches the full pod and container names, and the history chosen is fetched before following; pods that appear later start from their beginning, as stern does. Up to `logs.maxStreams` (50) containers are followed at once; the rest are listed as "not followed (limit)". The live filter (`/`) is fzf-like like the table's grep (space-separated terms all match, `!x` excludes, `^x`/`x$` anchor, `a | b` either, `"a b"` is one term), matched against the pod, the container and the text, so typing a pod or container name narrows to it (and `^` anchors to any of the three: `^GET`, `^api-1`); it hides non-matching lines (past ones too) and highlights the matches. `t` toggles RFC 3339 timestamps, `⌘K` clears the buffer (kept to `logs.buffer` lines, default 50 000), `⌘F` finds in the buffer as a terminal's find does (next/back, case, whole word, regex), `⌃D` or `⌃⇧D` closes the pane (there's no shell to claim `⌃D`). `↑`/`↓` scroll a line, `PgUp`/`PgDn` a page, `Home`/`End` (or `⌥↑`/`⌥↓`) go to the first/latest line, and `⏎` jumps back to the latest line; scrolled up, new lines don't move the view. The status line shows, as the table's "connected", a green dot and "following" while lines stream in (a grey one and "not following" once nothing is followed: started without follow, or every container ended), then shown/total lines, the container and pod counts, and any dropped or limited streams.
- Node shell (a privileged pod on a node, like ops-toolbelt's `hacks/ops-pod`): `⌥⌘O` from anywhere, or "node shell…" in the context menu of a node or pod row. The dialog's node is prefilled from the selected row (the node itself, or the node a pod runs on); the context's nodes are listed right away with pool, zone and state (not ready, cordoned), and `⏎` takes the prefilled one. The nodes come from the shared nodes watch: with a nodes pane open it's that pane's data, no extra list call; otherwise the dialog starts the watch, which stops 30 s after the dialog closed. Shared watches are keyed by the resolved resource, so `no`, `node` and `nodes` panes share one too. The image field shares its history with debug containers (default `opsPod.image`); the namespace completes from the context's namespaces (empty = `default`). Checkboxes: chroot into `/host` (the shell runs on the node; implies host root), host root (the node's `/` at `/host`) and host network; host root and host network are on by default. Optionally a persistent volume is mounted at a path (default `/pv`, not `/` or under `/host`): the field completes the context's PVs (from the shared `persistentvolumes` watch) and only bound ones can be picked, since a pod mounts a PV through its claim — so the pod then runs in the claim's namespace. The namespace and checkboxes of the last pod that started are remembered in `history.yaml` (across restarts) and win over `config.yaml`. The pod is named `ops-pod-kubewing-<user>-<hex>` (the OS login, as the script's `whoami`), privileged with host PID, bound to the node via `nodeName` (so cordoned and tainted nodes work; every taint is tolerated), and labelled `app=opspod`. "show YAML…" renders the pod kubewing would create as editable YAML; edited, that YAML is what starts (kubewing checks it stays a `v1` Pod on the prepared node with the `ops-pod` container, name and namespace — everything else, e.g. `priorityClassName` or extra volumes, is yours), `⌘⏎` starts it, `esc` goes back to the form. The shell opens in a pane below with node, pod, image and settings in its header; the pod is deleted when the shell ends, the pane is closed or the app quits (quitting waits up to 10 s for the deletes). See [Why a dedicated node-shell pod](#why-a-dedicated-node-shell-pod-vs-kubectl-debug-node) for how this differs from `kubectl debug node/…`.
- Edit (as `kubectl edit`): `e` on a row, or in its YAML view (`⇧E`: in a split to the right, the table staying as it is; `Esc` there closes it), reads the object fresh from the API server (not the watch) into an editable Monaco editor in the object view's place (from the table: over it, and closing the editor goes back to the table); it's modal: keys there are the editor's (`⌘D` deletes the line, `⌥↑`/`⌥↓` move it, `⌘[`/`⌘]` indent), only its own `Esc`, `⌘S` and `⌘⏎` reach the app, plus the keys that move the focus to another pane or tab (`⌘⌥` arrows, `⌘1`–`⌘9`, `⌘⇧[`/`⌘⇧]`, `⌃⇥`): the edit stays in its pane, marked `● unsaved`, and closing that pane or its tab asks first. `⌘S` (or `⌘⏎`) checks the edit and has the server dry-run it, then shows a side-by-side diff of the object now vs. as the server would store it (defaults and admission webhooks applied; both normalized as in the detail diff); `j`/`k` step through the changes, `⏎` writes it, `Esc` goes back to editing. Only what changed is sent (a JSON merge patch, field manager `kubewing`), with the opened `resourceVersion` as a precondition: if anyone changed the object since it was opened, the write is refused as a conflict instead of overwriting them. apiVersion, kind, name, namespace and uid can't change (that would be another object). Anything refused (invalid YAML, a webhook, a conflict, "no changes") shows below the editor with the text kept, also when the write itself fails after a confirmation. `Esc` with changes asks once; the second `Esc` discards them. Read-only contexts refuse `e` before the editor opens; `confirm: [edit]` in a context rule confirms after the preview (`Esc` there goes back to it). Server-side apply and recreate are still open (see [Plan.md](Plan.md)).
- Context menu: `⌘⏎` or right-click on a row lists every operation on it (view YAML, edit YAML, describe, diff, copy name, copy cell, jump to, exec, port-forward, delete) with its shortcut from the live keymap; view YAML, edit YAML and describe also show the key of their split variant (`⇧⏎`, `⇧E`, `⇧D`: in a split to the right), which `⇧⏎` on the entry runs. Unavailable entries say why ("needs a pod", "read-only context", "no change seen yet"). `j/k` move (wrapping around: `↑` on the first entry goes to the last), `⏎` runs, `Esc` closes. It's generated from [actions/registry.ts](frontend/src/actions/registry.ts): a new row action is one entry there, and a unit test fails for any `object.*` command that has none.
- Jump to related resources: `⌘J` on a row (also in its YAML view, or "jump to…" in the `⌘⏎` menu) lists where it leads (its **events** too, see above). **namespace**: "pods in shop" (the same resource narrowed to the row's namespace, filters kept; from an all-namespaces pane) and "namespace object shop" (the Namespace itself). **owners**: the `ownerReferences` chain up (pod → replica set → deployment, and on through Gardener and other CRD owners; the controller first, `↑` for each level), each looked up by metadata only and checked by UID, so a deleted or recreated owner is listed disabled with why. **owned**: what the row is known to run, counted: deployments → their replica sets and pods, replica sets, statefulsets, daemonsets and jobs → pods, cron jobs → jobs, nodes → their pods (all namespaces), namespaces → their pods, a custom resource with a scale subresource → the pods its `labelSelectorPath` selects. Direct children must also name the row in their `ownerReferences` (selectors can overlap); a deployment's pods go by its selector. Anything else (a CRD owning config maps) is found by "scan for owned…", which lists every resource type's metadata in the row's namespace and matches the owner's UID; it runs only on request, skips types it can't list (and says how many), and for a cluster-scoped owner (all namespaces) asks with a second `⏎` first. **references**: what the row points at other than by owner: a pod → the claims its volumes use (ephemeral volumes' too; a claim that doesn't exist is listed disabled), each followed by its bound volume, and all those volumes' attachments in one entry; a stateful set → its claims (by the controller's names, `o.metadata.name.matches("^(data)-db-[0-9]+$")`: every ordinal, so claims left behind by a scale-down count too), the volumes bound to them and their attachments, each one counted entry; a claim → its persistent volume (checked to exist; "not bound" while pending) and straight on to that volume's volume attachments, a volume → its claim (checked by UID) and its volume attachments, a volume attachment → its volume and node. Attachments are matched by `o.spec.source.persistentVolumeName == "pv-…"` (no field selector covers it), (`in [...]` for a pod's volumes), so counting them lists the volume attachments in full; a pod's or stateful set's claims are read in full too (one list in its namespace) to find their volumes. `j/k` move (wrapping around, as in the `⌘⏎` menu), `⏎` jumps in place, `⇧⏎` opens the target in a split below, `Esc` closes. A jump is an ordinary pane: the target is a source plus filters (`o.metadata.name == "web"`, `o.metadata.ownerReferences.exists(r, r.uid == "…")`, a label or field selector; a field selector shows as a chip in the header), so the filter bar shows why it holds what it holds, and clearing the filter shows the siblings. In place, the pane keeps a history as a browser does: jumps and `⌘O` to a new source (context, resource, namespace or selectors; the same source again is no step) are its steps, while filter edits are part of the view they're on and come back with it. `⌘←` goes back, `⌘→` forward again to the view just left (in the table; in text fields they stay start/end of line), each time with the row that view had selected again; a new jump or `⌘O` after going back drops the forward history. As in a browser, the header starts with `←` / `→` buttons, greyed out with nowhere to go (the tooltip says where each leads): a click goes one step, holding one (or a right-click) lists that history below it, the nearest view first, with its filters; `j/k` move, `⏎` or a click goes there at once (the views passed over go on the other history), `Esc` or a click elsewhere closes. Splits and new tabs start without history. Read-only (metadata only, no tokens): works on read-only contexts and on deleted rows (their last state).
- Events of a row: `v` (also in its YAML view), or the **events** entry at the end of the `⌘J` jump menu (counted; `⇧⏎` opens them in a split below, next to the row), turns the pane into the events about that object, as a jump does: `events` with the field selector `involvedObject.kind=Pod,involvedObject.name=web-0` (the chip in the header) in the object's namespace, all namespaces for a cluster-scoped one (a node's events are in `default`). By kind and name, not UID, as `kubectl describe` does: the kubelet records a node's events with the node's name as its UID. It's a live watch like any pane, so new events show up as they happen; `⌘←` goes back to the row. Works on deleted rows too (events outlive their object for a while), not on an event row. No events yet can still be jumped to (to watch for them).
- Cheat sheet: `?` (again or `Esc` closes) lists every shortcut by section, with the context a key is scoped to, read from the live keymap — so `keybindings.yaml` overrides show up as they are. It's built from [keys/cheatsheet.ts](frontend/src/keys/cheatsheet.ts), and a unit test fails for any command that no section mentions.
- Native menu bar (macOS app): File / Object / View / Pane / Tab / Help list the app's commands, built from the live keymap in [keys/appMenu.ts](frontend/src/keys/appMenu.ts); a click goes to the same dispatcher as a key. Global ⌘ chords are the items' native key equivalents (so `keybindings.yaml` changes show there too); scoped and plain keys appear in the label instead, so a menu shortcut never fires inside a text field. Wails' default menu is replaced: no ⌘W "Close Window" (⌘W closes a tab) and no ⌘R "Reload". Edit keeps ⌘C/⌘V/⌘A/⌘Z for text fields.
- One kubewing per config directory: launching it again from a terminal while one is running opens a new tab there and brings its window to the front; the new launch exits. The tab starts on that terminal's kubeconfig (`$KUBECONFIG`, else `~/.kube/config`), at its current context and the namespace that sets (pods, no filters), so a `gardenctl` shell's target opens as it is. kubewing reads that kubeconfig itself (the launch sends only the path, encrypted with `instance.key` in the config directory, so no other app can make it read one) and keeps it per tab: splits and `⌘T` from there stay on it, and `⌘O` lists its contexts (the dialog names the files). Context names alone don't say which kubeconfig they're from, so the same name in two terminals' kubeconfigs is two clusters, while safety rules match the name as always. `-new-process` starts a kubewing of its own instead (its own window, watches and shells). A different config directory (`-config`, `$KUBEWING_HOME`) is always a kubewing of its own.
- Launched from a terminal, kubewing detaches: the prompt comes back right away, and closing the terminal doesn't end it. It runs in a session of its own, logging to `kubewing.log` in the state directory (appended; at 10 MiB it moves to `kubewing.log.1`). `-wait` (or `launch.wait: true` in `config.yaml`; `-wait=false` overrides that) keeps it in the foreground with the log in the terminal, as handy while developing. Output that goes to a file or pipe (`kubewing 2>&1 | tee`) stays as it is, so does `-headless`; a launch handing over to a running kubewing (see above) exits either way, `-wait` included, and says so in the terminal (`-new-process -wait` for a foreground one of its own).
- Safety model, decided in Go: per-context label and color on pane frame, header and tab; read-only contexts refuse delete, exec, debug, node shells and edit (describe and port-forward still work); delete always asks, other actions per rule; strict rules require typing the object name. Every mutation goes through a single-use, 60 s token bound to the exact object. See [Safety rules](#safety-rules).
- Config in `~/.kubewing/` (or `$KUBEWING_HOME`), read-only and optional (the files kubewing writes there are its own `history.yaml`: recent debug container and node-shell images, the last node shell's namespace and checkboxes, and the window's size: it reopens in it, centred, maximised or fullscreen again; and `columns.yaml`: the columns and widths per resource type, plain YAML to edit by hand while kubewing isn't running); `⌘⇧,` reloads. Problems show in a banner and fall back to defaults. See [testdata/config/](testdata/config/) for a commented example:
  - `config.yaml`: theme, extra diff ignore rules, per-context safety rules (`match` glob, `label`, `color`, `readOnly`, `confirm` (`delete`, `exec`, `debug`, `opspod`, `edit`), `strict`; see [Safety rules](#safety-rules)), debug container defaults (`debug.image`, `debug.profile`), node-shell defaults (`opsPod.image`, `opsPod.namespace`, `opsPod.chroot`, `opsPod.hostRoot`, `opsPod.hostNetwork`; until a pod ran), terminal scrollback (`terminal.scrollback`, lines, default 10000), launching from a terminal (`launch.wait`: stay in the foreground, default false: detach), the recorded history's memory budget (`history.budget`, a size, 1Mi–64Gi, default 512Mi, counting the objects' newest copies and their changes; how far back history reaches follows from it; a reload (`⌘⇧,`) applies it to running recordings too, a lower one drops old versions at once).
  - `keybindings.yaml`: `{key: cmd+k, command: row.down, when: [table]}`; `command: none` unbinds.
  - `themes/<name>.yaml`: overrides for the tokens in [theme.css](frontend/src/theme.css).
- `⌘O` completion for resource and namespace: ranked by exact > prefix > substring > fuzzy over names, short names, singular and kind (`po`, `pdb`, `Deployment`). Suggestions show group, scope and short names, with the typed letters highlighted. A plural served by two groups (`machines`) completes to its qualified name. Fields open with their value selected; the list opens on typing (or `↓`). `⇥` completes, `⏎` completes and opens, `⌥↑/⌥↓` first/last suggestion, `Esc` closes the list, then the dialog. Unknown text still opens as typed.
- Cluster catalog (resource types + namespaces per context), fetched in the background once a pane is open on that context. It never connects on its own, so it never triggers exec-plugin logins. Refreshed by metadata-only CRD/namespace watches, on resolve misses and every 5 min. Partial discovery (an aggregated API down) and forbidden namespace lists degrade to a hint.

## Safety rules

Safety rules guard clusters against mistakes. They're per kubeconfig context and live in `config.yaml` under `contexts:` (`⌘⇧,` reloads them):

```yaml
contexts:
  - {match: "*-prod", label: PROD, color: prod, readOnly: true}
  - {match: "*-live", label: LIVE, color: changed, strict: true}
  - {match: "*-dev", label: DEV, color: added, confirm: [delete, exec, debug, opspod, edit]}
```

| Field | Effect |
|---|---|
| `match` | Required. A glob ([`path.Match`](https://pkg.go.dev/path#Match) syntax: `*`, `?`, `[a-z]`) on the context name. The first rule that matches wins, so put specific rules before broad ones. |
| `label` | A badge on the pane frame, header and tab, e.g. `PROD`. |
| `color` | Colors that frame, header and tab: a theme token (`prod`, `changed`, `added`, `deleted`, … from [theme.css](frontend/src/theme.css)) or a CSS color (`#f14c4c`, `rgb(…)`, `oklch(…)`). |
| `readOnly` | Refuses every mutation: delete, exec, debug, node shell (`opspod`) and edit (`e` is refused before the editor opens). Reading still works: tables, YAML, diff, describe, logs, jump to related resources, port-forward. |
| `confirm` | Actions that ask before they run: `delete`, `exec`, `debug`, `opspod`, `edit`. Delete always asks, also when it's not listed. An edit is confirmed after its diff preview. |
| `strict` | A confirmation needs the object's name typed, not just `⏎`. |

A context no rule matches is writable, and only delete asks.

Rules match the context name only, whichever kubeconfig it comes from: a tab that a launch from another terminal opened on `@1/shoot--foo-prod` (see above) gets the `*-prod` rule as well. A rule with an error (no `match`, a broken glob, an unknown color or action) shows in the config banner and is skipped, so its contexts fall through to the next matching rule or to the default. Check the banner after editing.

The rules are applied in Go ([internal/safety](internal/safety/safety.go)); the frontend only shows what Go decided. Every mutation is two-phase: Go checks the rule and issues a single-use token, valid for 60 s and bound to that exact action and object (kind, context, resource, namespace, name, UID), and the mutating call only runs when it redeems that token. So no path (keys, menu, context menu, palette) gets around a rule, and a confirmation can't act on another object than the one it showed. A config reload drops outstanding tokens, since they were issued under the old rules.

## Why a dedicated node-shell pod (vs `kubectl debug node`)

`kubectl debug node/<n> -it --image=…` does almost the same thing — it also creates a real pod bound to the node with host PID and the node's `/` at `/host`. For a generic cluster the two are interchangeable, and the node shell deliberately reuses that shape. The differences are Gardener-specific and are the reason ops-toolbelt's `hacks/ops-pod` exists:

- **Tolerates every taint.** The node shell sets `tolerations: [{operator: Exists}]`; `kubectl debug node` sets none. Binding via `.spec.nodeName` bypasses *scheduling* (so `NoSchedule` is moot either way), but `NoExecute` taints are still enforced by the eviction controller — so on a Gardener etcd pool (tainted `pool.worker.gardener.cloud/dedicated-for=etcd:NoExecute`) `kubectl debug node` gets **evicted**, the node shell stays.
- **Reaches the seed API server.** The pod carries `networking.gardener.cloud/to-seed-apiserver: allowed`; on a seed with network policies a plain debug pod can't.
- **No istio sidecar** (`sidecar.istio.io/inject: "false"`), so a mesh-enabled namespace doesn't inject one into the shell pod.
- **Auto-cleanup.** The pod is deleted when the shell ends, the pane closes or the app quits; `kubectl debug node` leaves it behind.
- **`priorityClassName`** (e.g. `system-cluster-critical`, to land on a full node) is offered as a commented line in "show YAML…" — uncomment it, or add anything else, before starting.

Everything the two share is unchanged: both need a privileged namespace (PodSecurity applies equally — the `default` namespace's `baseline` standard rejects host namespaces, hostPath and privileged, so use e.g. `kube-system`), and both can only mount a PV through a PVC in the pod's own namespace. kubewing's value is the UI around it (node/PV completion, the dialog, YAML preview/edit, the safety model, remembered settings, auto-delete) regardless of the underlying spec; the hand-crafted pod is just the right default for Gardener seed and etcd nodes.

## Tests

- **Goldens** (`internal/view/testdata/*.golden`): tapes in `testdata/tapes/` replayed through the view; frames compared to the golden files. They are the spec: change them only with `make golden-update` and review the diff on its own.
- **Properties** (`rapid`): sort doesn't affect markers, no-op relist produces no frame, rows never leave the viewport, no unchanged resends.
- **Bridge budget**: 10k objects under an event storm → ≤10 Hz, ≤ viewport rows per frame.
- **Catalog**: never connects to a context without an open pane; partial discovery stays usable; failed refreshes keep the last good state; CRD/namespace watches and resolve misses trigger refetches.
- **Frontend units**: keymap (incl. collisions, suppressed webview defaults), layout/command/MRU invariants under 3000 random ops, directional tile focus, cell layout, row cache, completion ranking/highlight goldens.
- **E2E**: Playwright drives Chromium; asserts via `window.__kw.inspect()` and ARIA snapshots.
